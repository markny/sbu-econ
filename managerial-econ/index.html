Start with Foundations, then read the numbered chapters in order. Each chapter closes with a study map and five public practice questions. Use the questions to test your reasoning; your course’s assignment instructions determine which work to submit.

The key-term index links back to explanations in the chapters. Two worked reasoning examples show how to move from a plausible claim to a defensible recommendation. They are public learning aids, not answers to the chapter assignments.

Use the examples actively

Before looking at a worked calculation, name the alternatives and identify what changes between them. After reading it, change one assumption and ask whether the recommendation survives. For a written recommendation, explain why the proposed arrangement changes someone’s choices and what it costs to operate.

A performance improvement is not automatically evidence of greater effort or proof that a new policy worked. Keep asking what else could explain the result and what information would change your decision.
