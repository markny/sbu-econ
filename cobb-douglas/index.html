Show the math: from output to wages and rents

Competitive firms pay each input its marginal product. Differentiating with respect to one input means holding the other input, technology, and the exponents fixed.

For labor, hold K fixed

MPL = (1 − α) A Kα L−α

W/P = MPL = (1 − α)Y/L

For capital, hold L fixed

MPK = α A Kα−1 L1−α

R/P = MPK = αY/K

The power rule: bring down the exponent, then subtract 1 from it. For labor, (1 − α) − 1 = −α. For capital, α − 1 remains α − 1.

The tangent is the derivative: the output change per unit for an arbitrarily small input change. Adding one whole worker gives a slightly different finite change.

What if all nominal prices double?
1.00

Keep the real economy fixed and change its price level. Nominal wages and rents adjust with the output price.

Nominal wage 0.500W = P × MPL

Nominal rent 0.500R = P × MPK

Real wage 0.500W/P = MPL

Real rent 0.500R/P = MPK

Doubling W, R, and P leaves W/P and R/P unchanged. The money amounts double; the quantities of goods those payments buy stay the same.

Model assumptions. Constant returns to scale, competitive factor markets, and inputs paid their marginal products. K is land in the medieval example and capital more generally. Technology changes here raise A, benefiting both factors. These are comparisons within the model, not forecasts of an economy’s adjustment over time.
