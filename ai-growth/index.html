Show the math and assumptions

The income threshold

Let s be labor’s income share and q be real output per worker. Average labor compensation is w = sq. Subscripts 0 and 1 mean before and after.

w₁ / w₀ = (s₁ / s₀) × (q₁ / q₀)

Break-even productivity growth = s₀ / s₁ − 1

For the time comparison, g₀ is annual growth without AI and T is the number of years. The annual growth rate with AI that matches the no-AI wage at T is:

g* = (1 + g₀)(s₀ / s₁)1/T − 1

A normalized CES economy

K = 100, L = 100, and initial output = 100. Let a be capital’s initial share, AK capital effectiveness, AL labor effectiveness, and ρ = (σ − 1)/σ.

Y = 100[a(AKK/100)ρ + (1 − a)(ALL/100)ρ]1/ρ

Capital share = a(AKK/100)ρ / [a(AKK/100)ρ + (1 − a)(ALL/100)ρ]

Real wage = MPL = (labor share)Y/L
Real capital rental = MPK = (capital share)Y/K

At σ = 1, use the continuous Cobb–Douglas limit: Y = 100(AKK/100)a(ALL/100)1−a. Income shares then remain a and 1 − a.

What the comparison includes

All values use a common output price. The main view is an income-accounting exercise; the CES view additionally assumes constant returns to scale, competitive factor markets, and marginal-product payments. Capital income is gross rental income, before depreciation—not an asset price or net investment return.

Employment, hours, and physical capital are fixed. Average worker compensation does not describe every worker, unemployment, household capital ownership, taxes, transfers, or differences between consumer and output prices. A larger average wage does not establish that every household is better off.
